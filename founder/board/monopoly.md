# Monopoly lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The shop is a reseller of a brand (Al-Jawhera Paints). If that brand is exclusive or strongly preferred in the region, that is a small market that could be owned first.
- A physical shop plus a website gives two ways to reach buyers.
- The owner must control the site and data, which avoids dependence on a platform that can change terms.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Distribution is unproven.** A good site with no traffic is not a business. Nothing in the plan says how the first 100 online customers arrive or what each costs.
2. **Nothing a copycat cannot copy.** A shop website is reproducible in weeks. Without exclusive stock, a brand people ask for by name, or know-how others lack, any rival or a larger retailer can match it.
3. **The market is wide and thin.** "All Iraq" is a large area for a single shop to serve. Delivery cost and reliability may erase the margin outside nearby governorates.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Is Al-Jawhera exclusive to Al-Erjiwan, or sold by others in Iraq?
- Which governorates does the shop already serve well? Start by dominating those.
- What does Ali know about paint, colour matching or local painters that online retailers do not?
- Who else sells paint online in Iraq today, and how many orders do they get?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Pick a first small market (for example Babil and neighbouring governorates) and prove it before promoting nationwide.
- Establish what is exclusive: the brand, the stock, tinting service, or local expertise.
- Write down how the first 100 customers will be reached (Instagram, WhatsApp, painters, existing customers).
