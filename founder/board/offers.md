# Offers lens
Score: 6/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The pain is real and already visible: customers ask about price, stock, colour and order status by hand.
- Paint is a repeat, high-ticket purchase, and colour choice is a known source of fear. A colour picker with a room preview attacks that fear directly (it raises the buyer's confidence they will like the result).
- Cash on delivery removes the main trust barrier for first-time online buyers.
- An existing shop means the owner already has stock and some customers.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No offer beyond "the same paint, online".** If the only difference is convenience, it competes on price, and anyone can match a price.
2. **Likelihood is weak.** A buyer must trust that colour on a phone screen matches the wall, and that delivery across Iraq arrives intact and on time. Nothing in the plan yet gives proof or a guarantee.
3. **Effort is still high for the buyer.** Choosing the quantity (how many litres a room needs) is left to the customer, and getting it wrong means a second order.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly is the buyer: homeowners, painters, or contractors? Each needs a different offer.
- What happens if the colour looks wrong, or a can arrives damaged? Is there a guarantee?
- Could the site tell a customer how many litres their room needs?
- Is there a reason to buy from Al-Erjiwan rather than the nearest shop, other than delivery?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Decide the target buyer.
- Add a clear guarantee for damaged or wrong-colour orders that the CFO can afford.
- Add a litres calculator or equivalent so the customer does not have to guess.
- Be able to say, in one line, why a customer in Basra or Erbil would order from Kefil.
